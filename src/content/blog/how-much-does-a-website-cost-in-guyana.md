**The short answer:** in 2026, a professionally designed small business website in Guyana typically costs **GY$300,000 to GY$500,000**. Larger business sites cost **GY$500,000 to GY$1M**, and custom builds with booking systems, product catalogues or calculators run **GY$1M to GY$2M or more**.

The price depends less on how many pages you have and more on what the website needs to *do* for your business. Here's how it breaks down, and how to compare quotes.

## Website prices in Guyana at a glance

| Type of website | Typical price (GYD) | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Small business website | GY$300,000 – 500,000 | Service businesses, contractors, salons, restaurants that need to look credible and get calls |
| Business website | GY$500,000 – 1,000,000 | Companies with several services or locations, a blog, and lead forms that need to rank on Google |
| Custom website | GY$1,000,000 – 2,000,000+ | Hotels, tour operators and firms that need bookings, catalogues, calculators or multiple languages |

These are the ranges we quote at In2ition Media for fully custom, hand-coded websites. Template-based sites can cost less (see below).

## What affects the cost of a website

### 1. Custom design vs a template
A template is a ready-made design with your logo and text dropped in. A custom design is built around your brand and your customers. Custom costs more, but it loads faster, looks like *your* business and is easier to grow.

### 2. What the site has to do
A brochure site that shows your services and a phone number is simpler than one that takes bookings, calculates quotes or manages a catalogue of products. Features like the [cooling estimator on Arctic Zone HVAC's website](/work/arctic-zone-hvac/) or the [booking flow for Singing Chef Adventures](/work/singing-chef-adventures/) add design and development time, and usually pay for themselves in enquiries.

### 3. Content and photos
Someone has to write the words and supply the photos. If you have them ready, the project moves faster and costs less. If you need copywriting or a photo shoot, budget for it.

### 4. Search engine optimization (SEO)
A website nobody can find doesn't bring in work. Good builds include on-page SEO from day one: page titles, descriptions, structured data and fast loading. Ongoing SEO, like content and Google Business Profile management, is usually a separate monthly service.

### 5. Languages and integrations
Multilingual sites, WhatsApp and email integrations, payment options and connections to other systems each add to the scope.

## Ongoing costs to budget for

Building the site is a one-time cost. Running it is not. Plan for:

- **Domain name**, renewed every year (.com or .gy)
- **Hosting**, so the site stays online and fast
- **Care and maintenance**: updates, backups, security and small content changes
- **Optional marketing**: SEO, social media and ads to send visitors to the site

Ask any designer to put these in writing before you start.

## Why some websites cost much less

You'll find offers for a website at a fraction of these prices. They're usually built from a template or page builder, reuse stock text and skip SEO. That can be fine to get started. The trade-off is a slower site that looks like many others and rarely brings in new customers from Google, which is the main reason most businesses want a website in the first place.

## Questions to ask before you hire a web designer

1. Is the design custom, or based on a template?
2. Who owns the website and the domain when it's finished?
3. What's included after launch, and what does it cost per month or year?
4. Is basic SEO included?
5. Can I see websites you've built for businesses like mine?
6. How is payment split?

### Are there monthly costs after the website is built?
Yes. Every website needs a domain name and hosting, and most businesses also pay for ongoing care such as updates, backups and security monitoring. Ask for these costs up front so there are no surprises after launch.

### How long does it take to build a website?
A small business website usually takes three to five weeks from the first call to launch. Larger or custom sites take six to ten weeks, mostly depending on how quickly content, photos and feedback come in.
