**The short answer:** create a free Google Business Profile, verify it, and fill in every detail: category, map pin, hours, services and photos. Then collect reviews from real customers and keep the profile active. That's what puts you in the Google Maps results when people nearby search for what you sell.

## What a Google Business Profile is, and why it matters in Guyana

A Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your business on Google Maps and in the box on the right-hand side of Google search results. It shows your name, location, phone number, hours, photos and reviews, with buttons to call you, get directions or visit your website.

When someone in Guyana searches "hardware store near me", "AC repair Georgetown" or "restaurant in Berbice", Google usually shows a map with three businesses above the regular results. That spot gets most of the calls. Many Guyanese businesses still don't have a profile, or have one that's half filled in, so a complete profile can put you ahead of competitors quickly.

Google says local results are based on three things:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone searched for.
- **Distance:** how close you are to the person searching, or to the place they named.
- **Prominence:** how well known your business is, including reviews, links and mentions across the web.

You can't move your shop, but you can control relevance and prominence. That's what the steps below are for.

## Step 1: Create or claim your profile

1. Go to [google.com/business](https://www.google.com/business/) and sign in with a Google account. Use a business account you'll keep, not a staff member's personal email.
2. Search for your business name. If it already appears, someone (possibly a customer) may have added it to Maps. Select it and follow the steps to claim it. If someone else already manages it, Google lets you request access.
3. If it doesn't appear, choose **Add your business** and enter your business name exactly as it appears on your sign.

Use your real business name only. Adding extra words like "Best AC Repair Georgetown" to your name goes against Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended.

## Step 2: Verify your business

Google needs to confirm you really run the business before your profile goes public. The options you see depend on your type of business. Common ones include:

- **Video recording:** a short, unedited video showing your location or signage, your tools or stock, and proof you manage the business, such as unlocking the door or opening the till.
- **Phone or text message** to your business number.
- **Email** to your business address.

Video verification is the most common option now. Plan it before you hit record: start outside so Google can see the street and your sign, walk inside, and show something only the owner or manager could access. Google then reviews the video, which usually takes a few days.

## Step 3: Choose the right categories

Your **primary category** is the single most important choice on your profile. Pick the one that describes your main business, as specifically as possible. You can add up to nine more categories for other things you do.

Some examples from Guyana:

| Business | Primary category | Extra categories |
|---|---|---|
| AC sales and repair | Air conditioning contractor | HVAC contractor, Air conditioning repair service |
| Guesthouse | Guest house | Hotel, Bed & breakfast |
| Tour operator | Tour operator | Travel agency, Sightseeing tour agency |
| Hardware store | Hardware store | Building materials store, Paint store |
| Web designer | Website designer | Internet marketing service, Marketing agency |

Look up the top three businesses in the Maps results for your main search and see which categories they use. It's a quick way to check you're not missing one.

## Step 4: Put your pin in exactly the right place

Many Guyanese addresses don't have a street number, or use lot numbers and landmarks instead. That's fine. What matters is that the **pin on the map sits on your actual building**. Drag it into place when you set up the profile and check it again on your phone.

Two tips:

- Add a **Plus Code** for your location. It's a short code from Google Maps that pinpoints any spot, even without a street address. Long-press your building in Google Maps to find it.
- If you **visit customers** instead of having them come to you, set up a **service-area business**. List the areas you serve, for example Georgetown, East Bank Demerara and East Coast Demerara, and hide your address.

## Step 5: Fill in everything

Profiles with complete information get more views and more calls. Fill in:

- **Phone number** that's answered during your hours, plus your WhatsApp number if you use it.
- **Website**, or a link to the most relevant page.
- **Hours**, including **special hours** for public holidays like Mashramani, Phagwah, Eid, Diwali and Christmas, so customers don't turn up to a closed door.
- **Services or products** with short descriptions and prices if you can share them.
- **Business description:** a few plain sentences about what you do, who you serve and where. Mention the areas and services customers search for, written naturally.
- **Photos:** your storefront from the street (so people recognise it when they arrive), the inside, your team, your work and your products. Real photos beat stock images every time.

## Step 6: Get reviews, the right way

Reviews are one of the strongest signals for both Google and customers. A business with 30 recent, genuine reviews will usually win the click over one with three.

- **Ask at the right moment:** right after a finished job, a good stay or a happy customer interaction.
- **Make it easy:** your profile gives you a short review link. Send it on WhatsApp or print it as a QR code at the counter.
- **Reply to every review**, good and bad. A calm, helpful reply to a complaint often impresses future customers more than the five-star reviews.

What not to do: Google's rules ban buying reviews, offering discounts or gifts in exchange for them, asking only happy customers, and reviews from staff or family. Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile suspended.

## Step 7: Keep it active

Google favours profiles that look alive. Once a week or so:

- **Post an update:** a new product, an offer, a finished project or an event. You can reuse your Facebook and Instagram content.
- **Add new photos.**
- **Answer questions** customers ask on your profile.
- **Check your hours** before every holiday.

## Connect it to your website

Your profile and your website work together. Use **exactly the same business name, address and phone number** on your website, Facebook page, Google profile and any directories you're listed in. Link your profile to your website, and make sure your website clearly says where you are and which areas you serve.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Google Business Profile free?
Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. Google asks you to verify that you run the business before your listing appears publicly.

### Why doesn't my business show up on Google Maps?
Usually because the profile isn't verified, the categories are wrong, the map pin is in the wrong place, or the business has fewer reviews and less information than competitors nearby.

### How long does it take to appear on Google Maps?
After you submit verification, Google's review usually takes a few days, though some businesses wait longer. Once verified, your listing can appear in searches straight away, and it ranks better as you add details and collect reviews.

### Can I be on Google Maps without a shop or office?
Yes. Businesses that visit customers, like contractors, cleaners or mobile mechanics, can set up a service-area profile. You list the areas you serve and keep your home address hidden.

### Should I pay someone to get me reviews?
No. Google's rules ban buying reviews, offering discounts or gifts in exchange for them, and reviews from staff or family. Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile suspended.
