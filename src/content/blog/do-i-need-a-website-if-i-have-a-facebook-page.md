It's one of the questions we hear most from business owners in Guyana: *"I already have a Facebook page and customers message me on WhatsApp. Why do I need a website?"*

It's a fair question. Facebook is free, your customers are already on it, and for plenty of businesses it's where the first sale happened. So let's be honest about what a website does and doesn't add.

## What Facebook does well

Facebook is excellent for staying in front of people who **already know you**. Your followers see your posts, share your specials and message you directly. For a new business with a small budget, it's a great place to start.

## Where Facebook falls short

The problems start when you want to reach people who *don't* know you yet, or when you want customers to take you seriously for bigger jobs.

**1. People search Google, not Facebook, when they're ready to buy.**
When a hotel manager needs an HVAC contractor, or a visitor is planning a food tour in Georgetown, they usually start with Google. A Facebook page can show up, but a well-built website with pages for each service gives Google far more to work with, and far more ways to find you.

**2. You don't own your Facebook page.**
Algorithm changes decide who sees your posts. An account can be restricted or hacked. Years of work can become invisible overnight. Your website and your domain name belong to you.

**3. A feed is hard to browse.**
On Facebook, your best work is buried under last week's posts. A website lets you decide what customers see first: your services, your prices, your best projects and your reviews, laid out clearly.

**4. Bigger clients check.**
Companies, NGOs and overseas customers often look for a proper website before they send an enquiry or sign a contract. Not having one can quietly cost you jobs you never hear about.

## When you can wait

If you're just testing an idea, selling mostly to friends and repeat customers, or you don't yet have a clear list of services, a Facebook page and WhatsApp Business may be enough for now. Spend your energy on getting customers first.

## When a website starts paying for itself

It's time to invest in a website when:

- You want customers who **aren't** already following you
- You're pitching to businesses, government or overseas clients
- You're answering the same questions again and again in your inbox (prices, location, opening hours, how to book)
- You want to run ads and need somewhere better than a Facebook post to send people

## The best setup: both, working together

Your Facebook page and your website shouldn't compete. Use social media to stay visible and start conversations, and use your website as the home base that explains everything, builds trust and turns interest into enquiries. Link them to each other, and make sure your website has a clear WhatsApp or call button so customers can still reach you the way they prefer.
