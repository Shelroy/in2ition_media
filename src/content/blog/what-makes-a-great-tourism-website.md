Tourism in Guyana is growing, and travellers are doing their research long before they land. For tour operators, guesthouses and restaurants, the website is often the **first conversation** with a guest who is still thousands of miles away.

When we designed the website for [Singing Chef Adventures](/work/singing-chef-adventures/), the food, music and heritage tours led by Eon, The Singing Chef, we focused on a few principles that apply to almost any tourism business.

## 1. Lead with the people, not just the place

Guyana's landscapes are beautiful, but a tour is an experience with a person. The Singing Chef homepage opens with Eon himself, guitar in hand, and a headline that cycles through what the tours are really about: *taste, culture, music, history*.

Travellers book people they feel they already know. Put your guide, host or chef front and centre.

## 2. Make every experience easy to compare

Visitors want to know three things quickly: **what happens, how long it takes, and what it costs.** Each tour on the site has a photo, a one-line description, a price per person and whether lunch is included, laid out like a menu.

If a guest has to message you just to find out the price, many of them won't.

## 3. Put reviews where decisions happen

A guest planning from abroad is taking a leap of faith. Reviews from past travellers reduce that risk. Instead of hiding reviews on a separate page, place them next to the tours and the booking button, where the decision is being made.

## 4. Design for phones first

Most travellers browse on their phones, often on hotel Wi-Fi or mobile data. That means:

- Pages that load fast, with properly compressed photos
- Text that's readable without zooming
- **Call and WhatsApp buttons that stay within thumb's reach**, since that's how most guests prefer to get in touch

## 5. Let the design match the experience

A food and culture tour should feel warm and inviting. For Singing Chef we used cream tones, a classic serif typeface and rich food photography. A jungle lodge, a dive operator or a luxury hotel would each call for something different. Your website's look should set expectations for the experience itself.

## 6. Make it findable

A beautiful site doesn't help if travellers can't find it. Clear page titles, descriptive text about each tour, and a well-maintained Google Business Profile with reviews all help you appear when someone searches for things to do in Georgetown or Guyana.

## The result

In Jessica and Eon John's own words, in their Google review: *"his suggestions and implementation have really helped our business grow significantly. Our website is now something we are really proud of."*
