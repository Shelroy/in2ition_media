## The goal

Amazon Cooling Inc. supplies air conditioning and refrigeration parts and equipment from 149 Church & Light Street in Georgetown, serving technicians, contractors and businesses who need the right part to get a system running again.

Their previous website was built as an online shop, but customers don't buy HVAC parts that way. They call with a model number, or send a photo of a label, and ask whether it's in stock. The site needed to match how the business actually sells.

## What we built

A bright, clean website that opens with "Keep Guyana cool." and a commercial Trane unit, with the address and phone number right under the main buttons. The homepage moves through the product categories, the brands Amazon Cooling carries, a few example parts, and a three-step explanation of how ordering works.

The products page lists all 18 categories with a live search, and each category links straight to a quote request with that category already filled in. The contact page holds the quote form, a map with directions, and answers to the questions customers ask most, such as which brands are available and whether they can buy online.

The quote form is protected from spam and goes straight to Amazon Cooling's inbox. The site is fast on the phones most customers use, and it's set up for search with the business's details, an FAQ and a clear section for every product category.

## Why it works

When an AC unit is down, people want to know one thing: do you have the part? Amazon Cooling's new site answers that quickly, shows the brands they stock, and makes it easy to send a list and get a price, without making anyone create an account or check out.
