## Built for guests who are still thousands of miles away

**A tourism website is often the first conversation with a guest who hasn't arrived yet.** Travellers planning a trip to Guyana compare tours, read reviews and check prices weeks or months before they land. If your website doesn't answer their questions, or is hard to use on a phone, they book with someone else.

That's why hotel and tourism websites need more than a nice homepage. They need to sell the experience, make options easy to compare and make booking feel safe.

## What we've learned building tourism websites

When we designed the website for [Singing Chef Adventures](/work/singing-chef-adventures/), Guyana's food and culture tour guide, we focused on a few principles that apply to almost any tourism business:

- **Lead with people, not just places.** Travellers book hosts and guides they feel they already know.
- **Lay out experiences like a menu.** A photo, a line of description, a price and a clear link.
- **Put reviews next to the booking button.** That's where a guest needs reassurance.
- **Design for phones first.** Most trip research happens on mobile.

We wrote about this in more detail in [What Makes a Great Tourism Website?](/blog/what-makes-a-great-tourism-website/)
